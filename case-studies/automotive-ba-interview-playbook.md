## 3. How I think: the seven-step method

### Step 1 — Clarify the business decision
Ask what management needs to decide or improve. Define the business outcome, affected customer or team, scope and time period.

### Step 2 — Understand the customer and operating context
Understand what the customer expects, what employees need to deliver, what systems are involved and what constraints exist.

### Step 3 — Map the current process
Trace the journey from start to finish. Record activities, inputs, outputs, owners, systems, approvals, handoffs, waiting points and exceptions.

### Step 4 — Establish the evidence
Identify the data required, its source, definitions, time period, completeness and limitations. Speak with the people who perform the work. Do not treat an assumption as a finding.

### Step 5 — Find and validate the cause
Separate symptoms from root causes. Segment the evidence, compare cases, look for patterns and test alternative explanations. Validate the explanation with stakeholders and process records.

### Step 6 — Recommend a practical change
Compare options by customer value, business impact, effort, risk, dependencies and feasibility. Define requirements, ownership and acceptance criteria.

### Step 7 — Measure and follow through
Agree the baseline, KPI definitions, owners, review cadence and post-change measurement. Check whether the change produced the intended outcome and adjust if it did not.

**Short version:** Decision → Customer/process → Evidence → Cause → Recommendation → Owner → Measurement.

## 5. Automotive interview questions — with strong, defensible answers

### Q1. What does a Business Analyst do in an automotive dealership?

A Business Analyst helps the dealership understand and improve business processes using customer, operational and commercial evidence. Depending on the assignment, that could mean analysing enquiry follow-up, test-drive conversion, quotation turnaround, inventory visibility, delivery milestones or service issue resolution. The role is not just preparing reports; it is clarifying the problem, validating its cause, documenting what needs to change and helping the business measure the result.

### Q2. Sales are declining. What would you do first?

I would not immediately recommend more marketing or ask the sales team to make more calls. I would first define the decline: which outlet, model, customer segment and period, and compared with what baseline? Then I would break the journey into stages—enquiries, contacted leads, appointments, test drives, quotations, bookings and deliveries. I would compare volumes and conversion between stages, segment by source and model where data allows, and check data quality. I would then investigate the stage with the clearest deterioration, speak to the relevant teams and test possible causes before recommending an intervention.

### Q3. Customer complaints are increasing. How would you analyse them?

First, I would validate whether complaint volume is increasing in absolute terms or relative to deliveries, service visits or customers served. I would classify complaints by category, outlet, model, journey stage, severity, repeat occurrence, age and resolution time. I would examine a sample of cases to understand the actual customer journey and handoffs. Then I would identify the main drivers, validate them with the responsible teams and recommend changes with an owner and a measure of success.

### Q4. How would you improve test-drive-to-booking conversion?

I would define the cohort and period consistently, then examine eligible enquiries, completed test drives and subsequent bookings. I would segment by model, lead source, outlet and customer type if the data is reliable. I would investigate factors such as customer requirement fit, vehicle availability, appointment experience, follow-up, quotation clarity and timing. I would not assume that one factor causes low conversion without testing it. I would recommend a focused intervention, agree how it will be measured and compare results against a suitable baseline.

### Q5. How would you reduce delivery delays?

I would map the order-to-delivery process and agree the start and end milestones. I would capture planned and actual dates for each milestone, delay reasons, dependencies, owner and exception status. I would identify where waiting time accumulates and whether the causes relate to documentation, approvals, allocation, preparation, customer coordination or another dependency. I would then prioritise the controllable causes and propose milestone ownership, exception alerts or clearer customer updates as appropriate.

### Q6. What if the data is incomplete?

I would state the limitation rather than create false certainty. I would assess which fields are missing, whether the missingness is concentrated in particular teams or stages, and whether another source can validate the information. I would agree a minimum reliable dataset, document assumptions and show how much of the analysis is affected. If the evidence is insufficient for a conclusion, I would present it as an open question and recommend how to close the data gap.

### Q7. What if a manager disagrees with your findings?

I would understand the manager's concern and revisit the evidence, definitions and assumptions. I would show the process, examples and data behind the finding, invite operational context and check whether an alternative explanation fits better. If the evidence remains uncertain, I would say so. The objective is not to win an argument; it is to reach a sound business decision.

### Q8. How would you prioritise multiple problems?

I would compare customer impact, business impact, frequency, urgency, risk, effort, dependencies and confidence in the evidence. I would separate urgent operational risks from longer-term improvement opportunities. I would make the trade-offs visible and agree priorities with the accountable stakeholders rather than choosing based on personal preference.

### Q9. How do you ensure a recommendation is implemented?

I would translate it into a clear action: what changes, why, who owns it, who is affected, what dependencies exist, when it is due and how acceptance will be checked. I would maintain an action and issue log, review blockers with owners and track the agreed KPI after implementation. A recommendation is not complete just because it appears in a presentation.

### Q10. What is the difference between a Business Analyst and a Data Analyst?

A Data Analyst primarily focuses on extracting, preparing and analysing data to produce insights. A Business Analyst focuses on the business problem and the change required: understanding stakeholders, processes, requirements, constraints and outcomes. There is overlap. A Business Analyst must be comfortable using data, but the analysis should serve a defined business decision.

### Q12. What if the business asks for a dashboard but the problem is unclear?

I would clarify the decision the dashboard is expected to support, who will use it, what actions they should take and how often. Then I would define the measures, data sources, owners, refresh frequency and exception thresholds. If those are unclear, I would propose a short discovery step before building. Otherwise, the business may receive a dashboard that looks useful but does not change a decision.

### Q13. How would you handle resistance from employees?

I would involve the people who perform the process early, understand why the current method exists and identify the practical burden of the proposed change. I would use process evidence rather than blame individuals, test a change with the relevant team where feasible and incorporate feedback. I would make responsibilities and expected benefits clear, while respecting required controls.

## 6. A live-style case: “Our dealership has too many unconverted enquiries”

**Interviewer:** We receive many enquiries, but relatively few become bookings. What would you do?

**My response sequence:**

**First — Clarify.**
- Which outlet and period?
- Which vehicle models and lead sources?
- What counts as a valid enquiry?
- What counts as a booking?
- Is the concern volume, conversion, speed or quality?

**Second — Map the funnel.**

Enquiry → Assigned → Contacted → Qualified → Appointment → Test drive → Quotation → Booking

I would confirm whether these stages match the dealership's actual process and whether each event is recorded consistently.

**Third — Request evidence.**

Minimum fields:
- Unique lead ID
- Enquiry date/time and source
- Outlet and model of interest
- Assignment and first-contact timestamps
- Contact outcome
- Appointment and test-drive dates
- Quotation date and amount, where appropriate
- Booking date or lost status
- Lost reason, next action and owner

I would check duplicates, missing timestamps, inconsistent status definitions and leads still in progress.

**Fourth — Analyse the leakage.**

I would calculate stage-to-stage conversion and elapsed time, then segment by lead source, model, outlet and time period where sample sizes permit. I would distinguish mature cohorts from recent leads that have not had enough time to convert.

**Fifth — Investigate, don't guess.**

If the biggest drop is before contact, I would investigate assignment and response processes. If the drop is after test drive, I would examine requirement fit, vehicle availability, customer feedback, quotation clarity and follow-up. These are possible explanations, not predetermined findings.

**Sixth — Recommend and measure.**

I would propose a focused change based on the validated cause, assign an owner and define a review period. Measures could include first-response time, appointment conversion, test-drive-to-booking conversion, lead ageing and lost-reason completeness. I would compare like-for-like cohorts and monitor unintended effects.

**Management output:** A one-page decision brief showing the verified problem, evidence, root cause, options, recommendation, owner, timing, risks and KPI review.

## 8. KPIs I should understand in automotive retail

| KPI | Illustrative definition | Interpretation caution |
|---|---|---|
| Lead response time | First meaningful response time minus valid enquiry time | Define business hours and valid leads |
| Contact rate | Leads successfully contacted ÷ eligible leads | Define successful contact |
| Appointment conversion | Appointments ÷ eligible enquiries in a defined cohort | Avoid mixing immature cohorts |
| Test-drive conversion | Completed test drives ÷ eligible appointments | State the denominator clearly |
| Test-drive-to-booking | Bookings attributable to test-drive cohort ÷ completed test drives | Define attribution window |
| Quote turnaround | Quote issue time minus complete requirement time | Exclude incomplete requests consistently |
| Order-to-delivery time | Handover date minus agreed order milestone date | Define start milestone and cancellations |
| On-time delivery | Deliveries on or before confirmed date ÷ deliveries due | Track date changes transparently |
| Issue resolution time | Resolution timestamp minus issue creation timestamp | Segment by issue severity |
| Repeat complaint rate | Customers/issues with repeat complaint ÷ defined complaint population | Define what counts as repeat |

A KPI without a reliable definition can create misleading comparisons. I would agree the formula, source, exclusions and owner before reporting it.
