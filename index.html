02

My decision logic

I look for the gap between need, offer and delivery.

I compare what customers need, what the market already offers, and what the operating model can reliably deliver. The unresolved gap—not the first idea—is what I investigate.

  1. i

    Frame the decision

    What must change, for whom, and why does it matter commercially?

    DECISION
  2. ii

    Read the real context

    Compare customer behaviour and workarounds with competitor offers and the process as it actually runs.

    CONTEXT
  3. iii

    Isolate the residual gap

    Find what current offers or workflows still leave unresolved—not just the visible symptom.

    GAP
  4. iv

    Test business fit

    Weigh customer value, differentiation, commercial logic and operational feasibility together.

    VIABILITY
  5. v

    Earn the recommendation

    Separate evidence from hypothesis, test critical unknowns, then act—or stop if the case is weak.

    EVIDENCE → ACTION

BUSINESS-MODEL LENS

Retail of the Future (ROTF)

Studied the strategic implications of Mercedes-Benz India's direct-to-consumer retail model, looking at how national inventory access and pricing transparency can change the customer journey and the role of the retail partner.

Potential advantages considered
  • Broader access to national inventory rather than only locally held stock.
  • More consistent national pricing and greater price transparency.
  • Potentially lower inventory-holding exposure for dealer partners.
Commercial trade-offs considered
  • Potential pressure on dealer margins under a changed retail model.
  • Less flexibility for local, on-the-spot discounting.
  • Greater reliance on after-sales service and customer experience to support retention.

Scope note: this was a study of the model and its likely operating implications—not implementation of ROTF or a measured commercial-impact project.

Business perspective

About

I look for the gap between how a business competes, what customers expect and what its operating model can reliably deliver.

At NexaHomes, I worked across growth and operations for a 100+ residential-client portfolio, combining client coordination, competitor benchmarking and post-sale follow-through. It showed me how market positioning only creates value when the organisation can deliver on its promise.

At Silver Star Mercedes-Benz, I examined the Retail of the Future model and its implications for inventory access, pricing transparency, dealer flexibility and customer experience. In independent problem research, I apply the same habit of looking beyond the obvious: investigate recurring friction, study workarounds and existing solutions, then isolate the gap worth validating.

Across these experiences, my approach is consistent: understand the context, test assumptions against evidence, assess the trade-offs and identify a practical next step. My MBA research adds a structured research foundation to this business and operational perspective.

What I want to contribute Help teams make better-informed choices about where to compete, what to improve and how to turn priorities into reliable execution.
