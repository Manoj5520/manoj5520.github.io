02

What it is

A method for connecting work that is often disconnected.

Agentic Incline is an independently developed, human-directed strategic intelligence and business transformation methodology. It is designed to help a founder or organisation move from an unclear business question to a researched diagnosis, a tested opportunity, a workable business design, an accountable delivery plan and a measured decision about what comes next.

Its scope is broader than competitor benchmarking or idea generation. It connects intelligence, strategy, innovation, business design, execution and evaluation into one repeatable cycle. It can be used with human-led work; AI is an enabling layer rather than a mandatory decision-maker.

What it is

A structured method for opportunity discovery, strategic diagnosis, venture and business architecture, controlled execution and continuous improvement.

What it is not

Not a standalone software product, certification, autonomous agent platform, guaranteed business outcome or replacement for established governance and operating systems.

Evidence before conviction

Distinguish observed facts, assumptions, hypotheses and unknowns.

Problem before solution

Understand the need, workaround and residual gap before building.

Commercial relevance

Connect customer value with viable economics and delivery realities.

Human accountability

Keep consequential decisions with authorised people.

Test before scaling

Use experiments and baselines to reduce uncertainty.

Learn and adapt

Revisit assumptions when market or operating evidence changes.

Method in practice

What happens at each stage.

Open a stage to see its activities, deliverables and the question that should be answered before moving forward.

01Intelligence DiscoveryUnderstand the environment and establish the evidence base.−
ActivitiesMarket and industry scan; customer and stakeholder research; competitor and substitute analysis; benchmarking; journey and friction mapping; internal capability review.
Deliverables
  • Scoped research brief and source register
  • Customer / competitor evidence map
  • Friction and opportunity map
Decision gateIs the business question clear and the evidence fit for diagnosis?
02Strategic InterpretationExplain what the evidence means and what matters most.+
ActivitiesRoot-cause analysis; market and segment attractiveness; competitor-positioning implications; assumption review; capability, constraint and risk assessment. SWOT, PESTLE or Five Forces are selected only when useful.
Deliverables
  • Strategic diagnosis and root-cause view
  • Assumption and evidence register
  • Priority scorecard and decision options
Decision gateIs the problem and strategic rationale supported well enough to act?
03Opportunity EngineeringConvert a meaningful problem into a testable opportunity.+
ActivitiesDefine user and unmet need; map workarounds; generate solution hypotheses; assess desirability, feasibility and viability; test differentiation and willingness to pay.
Deliverables
  • Opportunity brief and target-user profile
  • Problem / solution hypotheses
  • Experiment plan and test evidence
Decision gateIs there enough evidence to proceed with the opportunity?
04Business ArchitectureDesign how the proposition will create and deliver value.+
ActivitiesValue proposition and customer segments; business and revenue model; pricing; unit economics; channels; operating model; resources, capabilities and go-to-market plan.
Deliverables
  • Business model and service blueprint
  • Economics assumptions and risk view
  • Capability plan and implementation roadmap
Decision gateCan the organisation deliver the value at acceptable cost and risk?
05Intelligent ExecutionTranslate the design into controlled work and learning.+
ActivitiesPrioritise initiatives; assign owners; align dependencies and resources; coordinate approved tools and AI support; run pilots; manage milestones, risks, change and adoption.
Deliverables
  • Prioritised backlog and owner map
  • Milestone plan and pilot dashboard
  • Risk register and decision log
Decision gateIs delivery safe, owned, measurable and authorised?
06Evolution & ScalingUse actual results to decide what should happen next.+
ActivitiesCompare outcomes with baseline; assess adoption, retention, service and financial performance; refresh external intelligence; review unintended effects and learning.
Deliverables
  • Benefits-realisation review
  • KPI scorecard and learning log
  • Improve / pivot / scale / stop recommendation
Decision gateDoes repeatable evidence justify further investment or scale?

Where it can work

Use cases, not industry slogans.

The most credible starting point is a bounded business problem with accessible evidence, a named owner and an outcome that can be measured.

01

Startups & new ventures

Assess customer need, alternatives, opportunity hypotheses, business model and early go-to-market experiments.

02

Product & service innovation

Discover unmet needs, prioritise concepts, test desirability and shape the operating and launch model.

03

Market & competitive intelligence

Map segments, competitors, substitutes, positioning, market-entry options and customer friction.

04

Growth & business operations

Diagnose handoffs, service gaps, customer journeys, execution bottlenecks and improvement opportunities.

05

MSMEs, GCCs & business groups

Potential pilot areas include new services, regional market needs, process redesign and capability planning.

06

Enterprise transformation

Potentially connect strategy, innovation and delivery portfolios through defined governance and measured pilots.

Reduce strategy-to-execution gaps

Make the transition from findings and recommendations to owners, pilots and measured actions explicit.

Improve evidence discipline

Show which conclusions are facts, assumptions or hypotheses, and what would change the decision.

Prioritise opportunity quality

Compare user value, differentiation, feasibility, economics and risk before committing resources.

Support better learning cycles

Bring customer response, operating outcomes and refreshed market evidence back into strategic review.

05

Methods & tools

Tool-agnostic by design.

The framework defines the sequence and decision logic. Teams select methods and software based on the problem, available data, risk and capability.

Research & discovery

Interviews, surveys, observation, desk research, source registers, review analysis, customer journeys and competitor matrices.

Strategic analysis

SWOT, PESTLE, Five Forces, benchmarking, segmentation, value-chain analysis and root-cause techniques.

Opportunity & product

Jobs-to-be-Done, Design Thinking, concept tests, prototypes, Lean experiments and desirability / feasibility / viability assessment.

Business design

Business Model Canvas, service blueprints, pricing logic, unit-economics models, operating-model maps and roadmaps.

Analytics & reporting

Excel, Power BI, SQL, SPSS, Tableau, project trackers and KPI dashboards where suitable and available.

AI & workflow support

Approved AI assistants, document analysis, research assistance, task planning, workflow automation and human review.

Capability transparency: These are illustrative compatible methods and tools, not proprietary software bundled with Agentic Incline. Manoj's documented working exposure includes practical Excel, intermediate Power BI, basic SQL, beginner Tableau, SPSS for academic research and practical AI productivity tools. This does not imply advanced proficiency in every listed tool.

Measurement architecture

Measure the decision and the outcome.

KPI selection is use-case specific. The measures below are candidate indicators—not preset AIBF targets or claims of performance already achieved.

StageCandidate KPI familiesManagement question
01 · DiscoveryResearch coverage; source quality / recency; customer and competitor coverage; problem frequency and severity; evidence gaps.Do we understand the problem and the evidence well enough?
02 · InterpretationAssumption resolution; root-cause evidence; segment attractiveness; risk exposure; decision-cycle time.Are we solving the right problem, for the right reason?
03 · OpportunityHypothesis test completion; interview or experiment conversion; willingness-to-pay evidence; problem-solution fit signals.Is the opportunity supported by actual user evidence?
04 · ArchitectureGross margin; contribution economics; acquisition and payback assumptions; pricing evidence; delivery feasibility; resource requirement.Can the proposition work commercially and operationally?
05 · ExecutionMilestone reliability; cycle time; experiment velocity; adoption / usage; budget variance; issue closure.Are we delivering the approved plan safely and reliably?

Every KPI needs a clear definition, baseline, denominator, owner, measurement period and decision threshold. Do not treat all metrics as mandatory or invent numeric targets before a pilot establishes a baseline.

07

Agentic operating model

Agentic does not mean unchecked autonomy.

The agentic layer is designed to help organise goal-directed work, but authority, judgement and accountability remain explicit.

Intent & task planning

Translate the request into an objective, constraints, questions, tasks, owners and completion criteria.

Context & continuity

Carry relevant project context and decisions forward, with controlled access and the ability to correct stale information.

Tool orchestration

Choose approved research, analytics and workflow tools, verify their outputs and record dependencies.

Evidence lineage

Link claims to sources and distinguish observations, assumptions, hypotheses, interpretations and recommendations.

Human decision gates

Require authorised review for material choices, external commitments, sensitive data use and consequential actions.

Monitoring & adaptation

Compare actual results with baselines, log exceptions and use the learning to revise the next cycle.
